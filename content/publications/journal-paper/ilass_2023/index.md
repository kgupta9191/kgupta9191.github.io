abstract: |
     Aerosolized droplets emitted during respiratory activities play an essential role in spreading various viral respiratory infections. Although it is widely accepted that large amounts of these droplets are created by upper airway activity, the sites and mechanisms of aerosol generation remain unclear. Our pioneering study focuses on the atomization process of airway mucus at the vocal folds (VF). We used a simplified in vitro model of the human VF that emulates their oscillating movement during phonation. The primary atomization of an artificial mucus applied to the VF was observed using optical measurement techniques. This novel insight shows how the process of aerosol generation is dominated by the burst of bubbles spanning the glottis. Furthermore, we analyzed the influence of the different phonation parameters on the resulting droplet size distributions. Additionally, three-dimensional multiphase CFD (computational fluid dynamics) simulations were conducted to get a deeper insight into the formation and breakup of liquid bridges at the VF. These simulations allowed us to use a more realistic geometry and implement wetting and boundary conditions in a more physiologic manner than in the experimental setup. The extension and breakup of liquid films proceeds quite similar to what can be observed in the experiments.
