title: 'An approach to quantifying added mass from computational simulations of water entry of solid objects'

abstract: | 
     Water entry is a highly dynamic process in which a solid body impacts the water free-surface, generating complex fluid-structure interactions that influence its subsequent motion. A fundamental concept in this phenomenon is the added mass, which represents the additional inertia the fluid imparts to the body as it accelerates and displaces the surrounding water. Immediately after impact, the added mass plays a dominant role in the transient dynamics due to rapid fluid acceleration and strong hydrodynamic forces. Understanding its magnitude and time evolution is crucial for analyzing entry dynamics, as they directly affect forces and energy dissipation during the initial phase of water entry. For simple geometries like spheres and cylinders, theoretical studies based on potential flow theory provide analytical expressions that serve as useful benchmarks. However, for more complex geometries, particularly those with varying nose curvatures, no universal solution exists. In our study, using an in-house multiphase flow solver we performed CFD simulations of water entry of cylindrical solid objects at various nose curvatures and impact velocities. Then, the time evolution of the added mass, arising immediately post-water contact, was quantified by following a new approach, in which the instantaneous liquid acceleration is evaluated and normalized by the solid acceleration. Prior to its application to the water entry simulations, the new approach to quantifying the added mass was assessed and validated using potential flow problems, where its results were compared with the theoretical added mass values.
